AVATAR WORLD MODEL — CODEX HANDOFF
Working plan, 22 October 2026
Repository: https://github.com/PraxisOG/avatar-world-model
Purpose: planning handoff for a future Codex implementation session.
No implementation, model assembly, dataset inspection, or training is claimed here.

1. PROJECT GOAL

Eventually take external human video (including suitable YouTube footage) and
generate corresponding motor kinematics and first-person video for a large-scale
training dataset.

Bootstrap the translator with existing egocentric recordings that contain tracked
head, wrists/hands, and fingers. Replay those human movements on a simulated body,
render a fixed external camera and a head-mounted POV camera, and save synchronized
joint motion. Original external footage is not required.

First translator learning question: can it reconstruct an unseen movement in the
same fixed scene? The longer-term goal is a capable household robot, with this
data generator supplying useful training examples. After Phase 1, probe an
existing model using movement tools before committing to a large policy-training
architecture. This probe is distinct from the Phase 2 reconstruction baseline.

4. PHASE 1A — LOAD AND ASSEMBLE ONE ARM / ONE HAND

Read current AGENTS.md, DESIGN.md, docs/first-training-roadmap.md, and
docs/v0.1-experiment-contract.md. Preserve unrelated repository work.

Inspect both model trees:
- Include paths, meshes, units, joint names, actuators, tendons, constraints,
  defaults, collision groups, and left/right conventions.
- Existing Reachy wrist rotations versus the Shadow model's wrist joints.
  Define the attachment boundary deliberately; do not duplicate wrist motion.
- Hand attachment origin and rotation, palm orientation, finger axes and scale.
- Name collisions when importing two copies later.
- Controller assumptions about the original gripper's geometry and mass.

Build the smallest reproducible scene with one supported Reachy arm and one
Shadow Hand. Remove/disable the replaced gripper intentionally.
Retain source model joint limits and coupling constraints, or document deviations.

Smoke test loading, joint enumeration, wrist alignment, arm sweeps, and finger
opening/curling/pinching. Render a short preview.
Synthetic commands are acceptable for this assembly smoke test ONLY; they do not
satisfy the later human-recording replay gate.

Acceptance:
- Scene loads reproducibly with pinned assets.
- The hand is correctly oriented at the wrist.
- Requested joint changes visibly affect the intended joints.
- No obvious attachment penetration or duplicated wrist transform.
- Model constraints/couplings are understood and respected.
Report blockers before choosing another embodiment.

5. PHASE 1B — INSPECT ONE REAL SOURCE SAMPLE

EgoDex is the existing candidate, not a verified selection.
Inspect official documentation and a small actual recording before bulk download.
Confirm:
- Head, wrist/hand, and individual finger pose channels.
- Whether pose labels are measured, fitted, or otherwise estimated.
- Coordinate frames, units, handedness, rotations/quaternion convention.
- Frame timestamps, sampling rates, tracking validity/confidence.
- Camera calibration and relation between tracked head and source camera.
- Official dataset split, license/access terms, and derivative-use permissions.

Choose a short, mostly stationary, contact-free movement with useful finger
variation. Preserve recording/session identity and source hashes.
Never use the official test split to train.
Do not infer data availability from an old reference table.

6. PHASE 1C — RETARGET ONE RECORDING

Start with one arm, wrist, and finger sequence. Define world/body/palm transforms
explicitly. Account for human/robot scale and different hand proportions.
Use position/orientation or fingertip objectives suited to the supplied channels;
do not copy human finger angles blindly into robot joints.

Preserve joint limits and coupled joints; log unreachable poses, tracking
residuals, filtering, interpolation, and rejected frames.
Maintain temporal continuity and measure thumb/fingertip errors.
Inspect self-intersection and arm/hand collision, even without object contact.

After this works, add the second arm/hand and head motion.
A fixed body cannot reproduce arbitrary head translation. Choose compatible
recordings and quantify unsupported motion; do not silently omit it or introduce
unplanned torso/base movement.

7. KINEMATIC REPLAY VERSUS DYNAMIC CONTROL

First prototype may set joint configurations directly and render them.
That establishes geometric reconstruction, not physically executable control.

Record source poses, solved joint configurations, and simulator-read state
separately. In kinematic mode, simulator state may equal the assigned configuration;
do not describe that as measured motor tracking performance.
Mark every episode replay_mode=kinematic.

If dynamic control is later added, record controller targets, actual state,
velocities, gains, control intervals, and target-to-state errors separately.
Do not invent motor commands, torques, or force labels from kinematic replay.
Physical Reachy/Shadow integration is outside this phase.

8. PHASE 1D — CAPTURE AND VERIFY ONE EPISODE

At each capture instant, freeze the state, read joint configuration, and render
external and head cameras without advancing the simulation between them.
Store:
- External RGB and head-camera RGB.
- Joint names/order, units, joint states, and solved targets.
- Original head/hand/finger poses, validity and retargeting residuals.
- Simulation time, source time, step IDs, sampling/interpolation rules.
- Camera intrinsics, parent frames, mount transforms, per-frame world poses.
- Model revisions, scene configuration, source provenance, replay mode and seed.

Use inspectable raw files first: JSON manifest, numerical tables, image streams.
LeRobot-compatible export can follow a validated raw format.

Validate actual image content and reload/replay, not just matching timestamp IDs.
Produce source-versus-replay pose plots and an external/POV preview.
Report positional/orientation errors, fingertip errors, jitter, limit violations,
and rejected samples. Establish explicit tolerances based on source quality and
the probe before accepting the replay; do not claim invented thresholds passed.

PHASE 1 DONE:
One real short recording drives both arms, articulated fingers, and the head;
the paired images and joint data reload correctly; limitations and measured errors
are documented. Review this evidence before scaling.

9. PHASE 2 — FIRST TRAINING RUN (PLAN ONLY UNTIL PHASE 1 PASSES)

Generate a small pilot dataset in the same fixed scene and camera arrangement.
Split by original recording/session before clipping, rendering variants, or
augmentation. All derivatives remain in the same split.

Baseline: modest pretrained image encoder plus a temporal model.
Input: external RGB clips only.
Target: joint configuration visible at the corresponding timestamps.
No source tracking, ground-truth POV, joint state, or simulator-only information
may leak into input features.
Specify whether clips are causal; offline reconstruction may use future frames,
but evaluate consistently and do not imply online control.

First overfit one short training sequence using the real saved-data loader.
Then train on the pilot set, tune on validation, and evaluate held-out movements.
Render POV by replaying predicted joints in the known scene.
This is NOT direct neural POV generation.

Compare against constant-pose and nearest-training-motion baselines.
Report joint, head/hand/fingertip, temporal, and rendered-POV errors.
Save checkpoint, split manifest, configuration, seed, versions, timing, and
side-by-side videos. Finite loss alone is not evidence of success.

photorealism, physical hardware, RL, or a large model.

12. LIVE DESKTOP DASHBOARD — REQUIRED PHASE 1 DELIVERABLE

The user wants to watch the simulation as it works, rather than watch a terminal
log. Provide a lightweight dashboard accessible on the Linux Mint desktop.
A local browser UI served on localhost is an acceptable default; choose the
simplest implementation that integrates with the replay process.

Build the basic viewer early, with the one-arm/one-hand integration probe, and
extend it as the full scene becomes available. Do not postpone visualization
until the first training run.

Required presentation:
- Large side-by-side external-camera and head-mounted POV panes.
- Live updates from actual simulation/replay output, not prerecorded demo loops.
- Clearly identify live replay, paused state, and saved-episode playback.
- During the one-arm probe, label POV unavailable until the head camera exists;
  do not present a substitute camera as true head POV.
- Compact status: current stage, recording/episode name, frame/time progress,
  preview FPS, recording status, and concise warnings/errors.
- When measured, show head/hand/fingertip tracking errors and limit violations;
  unavailable metrics must be marked unavailable, not fabricated.
- Play/pause, single-step, restart current replay, and playback-speed controls.
  Speed changes affect wall-clock playback, not source timestamps or saved labels.
- A short expandable event log for details, without overwhelming the camera views.

Engineering constraints:
- Display external/POV images from the same capture step; show their timestamp.
- Reuse captured frames rather than creating an independent simulation state.
- Decouple UI refresh from capture so a slow dashboard does not corrupt alignment
  or drop required dataset frames. Preview frames may be skipped as matched pairs.
- Keep preview buffers bounded; show stale/disconnected status if updates stop.
- Keep dataset generation usable without the dashboard (headless mode).
- Provide one documented launch command that starts the local dashboard and
  replay workflow, and prints its address. No cloud service or login required.
- This is a monitoring/playback interface, not a separate major web-app project.
  No remote access, elaborate editing tools, or training-control suite required.

Acceptance:
On the Mint desktop, the user can watch synchronized POV/external views during
a real replay, pause/step/restart it, and see progress or an actionable error.
Verify playback controls preserve saved time alignment and closing the viewer
does not silently destroy a capture. Report actual refresh performance; do not
claim an unmeasured real-time frame rate.

13. LATEST DECISIONS — STEREO, MODEL TOOLS, AND LATER RL

These additions preserve the Phase 1 acceptance gate and the external-video
translator experiment. They do not authorize a large training run in the initial
implementation session. Phase 1 remains one faithful replay plus the dashboard.

Stereo capture:
- Make the head camera rig configurable for synchronized left/right cameras,
  with a documented baseline, intrinsics, head-relative mounts, and per-frame
  world poses. Keep a designated primary POV for the existing data contract.
- Add the second camera after the basic head-camera replay works. It is an
  incremental capture option, not a blocker for accepting the first replay.
- Save left and right frames separately at the same frozen simulation state as
  the external frame and joint labels. Stitch only at model-input/display time.
- Optionally export metric simulator depth with its coordinate convention,
  invalid mask, and units for supervision/evaluation. Keep it out of the
  external-RGB-only translator inputs.
- The dashboard should allow selecting primary/left/right/stereo POV without
  compromising synchronized capture or requiring a major UI project.
- Two images supplied to a general vision model do not establish learned stereo
  depth. Later compare monocular, two-image, and stitched-pair inputs under a
  controlled resolution/token budget; measure reaching accuracy and latency.
  Stereo-specific training is a later experiment, not assumed functionality.

Existing-model control probe, after Phase 1 evidence review:
- Before choosing a large robot-policy training scheme, test an existing
  vision-language/tool-capable model against the simulator. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is
  a candidate to verify and benchmark, not a required dependency or selected
  final backbone. Use configurable model endpoints; the model may run on a
  server while the simulator/dashboard runs on the Mint desktop.
- This is a separate experiment from reconstructing joints from external video.
  Policy observations are head RGB (optionally stereo), instruction, robot
  proprioception, and action results/history. Simulator ground truth used for
  scoring must not silently appear in visual-only policy observations.
- Expose a small typed interface: head orientation, left/right palm 6-DoF
  targets, finger configuration targets, observe/status, and stop/cancel.
  Specify reference frames, units, absolute versus relative targets, duration,
  bounds, execution status, and timestamps.
- Use constrained IK to resolve arm/neck configurations, with joint limits,
  continuity, collision checks, and explicit residuals/unreachable-target
  errors. Palm poses do not encode fingers; retain finger commands and source
  finger data. Do not require the model to emit every joint at every frame.
- Start with bounded, interruptible commands. A hand-close primitive may specify
  finger motion; do not promise contact stopping or force regulation without
  implemented sensing/estimation and a validated controller.
- First proposed task: reach a visible target and correct when it moves.
  Measure success, endpoint error, correction behavior, invalid calls, and
  end-to-end observation-to-action latency. Separate kinematic task success
  from dynamically executable robot control.
- Log observations, tool arguments, actual outcomes, failures, and timing.
  The initial probe uses existing model weights; it is not RL or fine-tuning.
- Model-written reusable routines/macros are a later extension. Test in
  simulation through the same bounded API, keep them interruptible, and give
  them explicit success/failure checks. Do not execute unrestricted generated
  code inside the simulator process.
- Server-side model inference is compatible with the goal. Future hardware
  still needs local motor loops, limits, and a disconnect watchdog; those
  do not require an onboard large language model.

Training direction, deferred:
- Distinguish reconstruction training (external video -> motion), imitation
  training (observation/instruction/history -> actions), and interactive RL
  (actions -> simulator consequences -> rewards -> weight updates).
- The small translator baseline remains useful without locking the eventual
  policy to raw joint-angle outputs. Preserve both source head/palm/finger
  trajectories and embodiment-specific joints for later action representations.
- Use the existing-model probe to identify what actually needs training.
  Adapting a pretrained model is a candidate; a rented-compute large-model run
  is a long-term option, not a committed parameter count or immediate task.
- Recorded examples can support supervised training without a live simulator.
  Interactive simulator RL requires resets, an observation/action interface,
  rewards, termination conditions, reproducible seeds, and evaluation episodes.
- Kinematic pose assignment is insufficient evidence for contact-rich skills.
  Before grasping RL, validate actuator dynamics, collision/contact handling,
  friction, object response, and available force/contact observations.
- Introduce failures and recovery scenarios deliberately; score task outcomes
  and violations, not just plausible movement or language descriptions.
  Keep reward-only simulator information separate from deployable observations.
- Complete chores eventually require long sequences, task progress, outcomes,
  interruptions, and recovery examples. Isolated motion clips are only a start.
- Benchmark simulator throughput and visual-model inference/training costs
  before scaling parallel rollouts or renting compute. Synthetic performance
  does not establish transfer to real cameras, contacts, or hardware.
